The project’s main variable is whether a decision was covered in the media. The rules on this page must be kept in mind when reading the figures.

Definition

A decision counts as “covered in the media” if it has at least one verified news item in the national media within its window. The window opens 7 days before the decision date and closes 30 days after the decision’s publication day. The 7 days before the decision date absorb date errors in matching.

Publication day

The publication day is taken from these sources in order: the publication day on the Court’s website; failing that, the Official Gazette publication date; failing that, the Official Gazette line in the decision document. If none is found, the publication day is estimated by adding the median delay for the decision type to the decision date (55 days for individual applications, 75 for constitutionality review and 56 for political party decisions; High Court decisions use the constitutionality review median). For political party decisions the publication day is the Official Gazette date. The 3,671 decisions with an estimated publication day are flagged in the frame.

Frame

The frame consists of decisions dated between 23 September 2012 and 14 October 2026; 77 decisions without a decision date and 2 duplicate records are left out. The frame has 19,794 decisions; the first is dated 27 September 2012 and the last 8 September 2026. In this frame, 829 decisions were covered in the media (596 individual applications, 202 constitutionality reviews, 29 political party and 2 High Court decisions), and 18,802 decisions have no news item at all. Decisions on the financial audit of political parties are not part of the frame or of this count.

What is not treated as coverage

Three situations do not count as “covered in the media” and are kept separate:

  • Decisions mentioned only after the window closed (148 decisions). These serve a secondary “ever mentioned” analysis.
  • Decisions with news only before the decision date (15 decisions), for example those that made news at the hearing or application stage but not after the decision.
  • News agency output, the Court’s own announcements, local press, and duplicate content. These stay in the archive but are not counted.

Open window

For decisions published after 14 September 2026, the window had not yet closed on 14 October 2026, the last day of the news archive. These 207 decisions are included in the counts above; they are flagged in the frame and kept out of the main analysis.

Full count

On 15 October 2026 the project moved from a sample to a full count: all decisions in the frame will be coded with the codebook. The 500-decision pilot sample drawn on 8 October 2026 (250 covered and 250 not covered decisions) remains as pilot coding.

Decisions in the main analysis

The main analysis uses decisions whose window has closed. The 207 decisions with an open window and the two groups not treated as coverage (148 and 15 decisions) stay outside the main analysis.

One decision, several news items

For covered decisions, the number of news items, the number of independent sources, and the delay of the first news item from the decision date are recorded separately. The news count includes duplicate items. The independent source count treats a news family as one. These two numbers need not be equal.

Consequence of the full count

In a full count, counts are computed directly from the frame; no sampling weights are needed. In the pilot sample, half of the decisions were drawn from those covered in the media; this is a feature of its design and does not reflect the frame.
