The project’s main variable is whether a decision was covered in the media. The rules on this page must be kept in mind when reading the figures.

Definition

A decision counts as “covered in the media” if it has at least one verified news item in the national media within 180 days after the decision date. A 7-day tolerance applies to the news date: a news item dated up to 7 days before the decision date also falls within the window. This margin absorbs date errors in matching. The sampling frame is the 17,776 decisions dated between 23 September 2012 and 28 September 2025 (the first decision in the frame is dated 27 September 2012 and the last 25 September 2025). In this frame, 637 decisions were covered in the media. The 16,893 decisions with no news item at all form the control pool.

Cases not treated as coverage

Three cases do not count as “covered in the media” and are kept separate:

  • Decisions mentioned only after 180 days (218 decisions). These serve a secondary “ever mentioned” analysis.
  • Decisions with news only before the decision date (28 decisions), for example those that made news at the hearing or application stage but not after the decision.
  • News agency output, the Court’s own announcements, local press, and duplicate content. These stay in the archive but are not counted.

Sample

The analysis uses a case-control sample of 250 covered decisions and 250 decisions not covered. Controls are drawn from four periods (2012-2015, 2016-2019, 2020-2022, 2023-2025), and in each period the number of controls equals the number of cases in that period (30, 65, 72, and 83). Decision type and outcome are not matched; they are kept as variables in the model. The sample was drawn on 8 October 2026 with a fixed seed. In the control verification, records found to be mentioned in the archive were replaced with reserves from the same period. The seed and the sample size did not change.

Number of decisions in the model

For the 28 cases where Official Gazette publication took place after the first news item, the publication information is coded as missing by rule. These decisions stay outside the main model, which is built on 472 decisions. Two alternative codings that include the same decisions are reported separately as sensitivity analyses.

One decision, several news items

For covered decisions, the number of news items, the number of independent sources, and the delay of the first news item are recorded separately. The news count includes duplicate items. The independent source count treats a news family as one. These two numbers need not be equal.

Consequence of the case-control design

In this design, the share of cases in the sample (50 percent) does not reflect the share in the population: 637 of the 17,776 decisions in the frame were covered in the media. For this reason, only odds ratios are interpreted in the analysis.
