This map shows which topics are discussed by which groups of accounts in X (Twitter) posts about the Constitutional Court. Each dot represents an account and the navy markers represent topics whose names are masked. Accounts that posted on a single topic sit in the disc around that topic, while accounts that posted on several topics sit in small clusters between those topics. The width of a line between two topics is proportional to the number of accounts that posted on both.

Sentiment shares are percentages of the posts matched to the topic. Because a post can match more than one topic, the rows add up to more than the total number of posts.

Anonymity

The map and the table are anonymous. Account names, display names, post texts and like, follower and view counts are not published; the order and position of the dots are set independently of account identity. Topic names are also masked; topics are numbered by the number of accounts that posted on them (Topic 1 has the most accounts). Topics named after individuals and topics on which fewer than 10 accounts posted are not included in the map.

Data and method

The map is based on 15,379 X posts collected between 29 October 2024 and 6 November 2026 that were labelled as relevant and carry no spam or bot flag. Of these, 6,302 posts matched at least one topic and came from 3,538 accounts; 572 accounts posted on more than one topic. Topics were identified by keywords matched at the start of words in the post text. The colour of a dot shows the most frequent sentiment label among the account’s posts.

Keyword matching is approximate: a word can also occur in an unrelated sense, and a topic can be referred to in other words. The map therefore describes the relative size and overlap of topics; account counts should not be read as exact measurements. The period covered by the X data differs from the decision frame; see Data › How to Interpret the Data.
