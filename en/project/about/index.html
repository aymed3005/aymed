AYMED (“Factors Determining the Media Visibility of Constitutional Court Decisions in Türkiye and a Model Proposal to Strengthen the Court’s Communication with the Public”, TÜBİTAK 3005, Project No 225K477) is a research project on the extent and manner in which the decisions of the Constitutional Court of the Republic of Türkiye (the Court) appear in the media. The project is supported by TÜBİTAK under the 3005 Innovative Solutions in Social and Human Sciences Research Projects Support Programme, with Project No 225K477. It began on 23 December 2025 and will run for 18 months. The host institution is the Department of Political Science and Public Administration, Faculty of Economics and Administrative Sciences, Bursa Uludağ University.

The question

The Constitutional Court issues thousands of decisions every year. Which of them become news, and which do not? Do the type of decision (individual application or constitutionality review), its outcome, the deciding body, publication in the Official Gazette, and the language and subject of the text explain the difference? The project aims to answer this by linking two sets of data: a decision archive built from post-2012 decisions and a news archive covering the national media.

Two concepts

The study rests on two theoretical frameworks. Under the open justice principle, the reasoning and the process behind a decision should be open to public scrutiny. Mediatization theory examines how media logic (dramatization, personalization, conflict, simplification) shapes the communication of institutions. The project codes the Court’s decisions against the criteria of both frameworks and tests how these criteria relate to media coverage.

Three datasets

The project is built on three archives: a Court decision archive of 23,459 decisions (frame: 19,794 decisions dated between September 2012 and September 2026), a national media archive of 29,714 records, and an X (Twitter) dataset of 10,512 posts. How these archives were built, and what they do not cover, is explained on the pages under the Data menu.

Who it is for

The project has four target audiences: the communication units of the Constitutional Court and other judicial institutions; the bodies that set public communication policy (the Presidency’s Communications Directorate and the Ministry of Justice); journalists who report on the judiciary; and researchers in law, political science, and communication. The last work package delivers a communication strategy proposal based on the findings and a two-day workshop.

Resources provided

The site presents the 19,794 decisions in the frame in a filterable table (Decisions) and documents the datasets and the rules for determining media coverage (Data). Research results will be shared on this site after the interim and final reports are submitted to TÜBİTAK. The project’s institutional page at Bursa Uludağ University: uludag.edu.tr/aymed.
